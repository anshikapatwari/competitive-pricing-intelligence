Amazon.in · Competitive pricing analysis

How do headphone brands compete on price and discount on Amazon India?

An analysis of public Amazon.in listing data covering price, list price (MRP), discount depth, rating and rating count. It compares brands and categories, and tests whether deeper discounts go together with more customer ratings.

Key findings

    All figures are computed from a single dated snapshot. They describe associations between listings, not the effect of changing a price.

    Do deeper discounts go with more ratings?

    Association, not causation. This dataset is one snapshot with no time dimension, so it cannot show whether a price cut changed demand. A negative correlation is consistent with retailers discounting less popular items; it does not show that discounting reduces ratings.

    Methodology

    Data

    • Source: the public "Amazon Sales Dataset" on Kaggle, listing products from Amazon.in. Rows are one product each, with price, list price, discount, rating, rating count and category path.
    • The snapshot date is derived from the timestamp embedded in the product links and is shown in the overview. The data has no history, so no trends over time are claimed.

    Cleaning

    • Currency symbols and thousands separators are removed from prices and rating counts, and discount percentages are parsed from text.
    • Repeated product identifiers are collapsed to one row per product so that a listing is not counted twice.
    • The stated discount is checked against the discount recomputed from price and list price. Rows with unparseable values are excluded from the affected statistic only.

    Analysis

    • Medians are used throughout because price and rating-count distributions are heavily skewed.
    • Brand is taken from the first word of the product title and normalised for spelling. Brands with fewer than three headphone listings are grouped as "Other brands".
    • Associations use Spearman rank correlation, with 95% intervals from a bootstrap of 2,000 resamples. Rating count is log-transformed before correlation.
    • Correlations are also computed within sub-categories to avoid mixing categories that differ in both discount level and popularity.

    Limitations

    • Small headphone sample. There are only 63 unique headphone and accessory listings, so brand-level figures rest on a handful of products each.
    • Accessories included. The headphone sub-category also contains adapters, cases and ear pads, not only earphones.
    • Single platform, single date. There is no cross-platform comparison and no time series in this dataset.
    • List price is seller-reported. A high discount may reflect an inflated MRP rather than a real saving.
    • Rating count is not sales. It reflects cumulative reviews over a product's lifetime.

    Tooling

    • Cleaning, analysis and chart data are produced by a Python script in the project repository, and the page is generated from its JSON output. The repository also contains a scraper for collecting live prices, which was not used for any figure on this page.

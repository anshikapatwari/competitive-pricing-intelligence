80 products · Flipkart and Amazon.in · Daily collection

Do deeper discounts increase demand for earbuds, or only reduce margin?

This project tracks 80 earbuds across Flipkart and Amazon.in on a daily basis, recording price, MRP, rating and review count for each listing. A before-and-after event study is applied to every discount change to estimate whether the reduction was followed by a measurable increase in demand.

Key findings

    Findings are generated from the loaded data and appear only when the sample is large enough to support them. Review velocity is a proxy for demand rather than a measure of sales. Please refer to the Methodology section for the full set of limitations.

    Methodology

    Data collection

    • A curated list of 80 product pages across Flipkart and Amazon.in is used. No crawling or search-result scraping is performed. Requests are sequential, with a randomised pause of 3 to 6 seconds per host.
    • The robots.txt file is fetched and enforced for every URL using a wildcard-aware matcher. If the file cannot be retrieved, the URL is not scraped.
    • The User-Agent identifies the project and includes a contact address. Login walls, CAPTCHAs and paywalls are never bypassed.
    • Three consecutive blocking responses (403, 429, 503 or CAPTCHA) halt collection for that platform, and the reason is logged. Blocking is respected and not circumvented.
    • Only public, product-level data is stored: price, MRP, rating, review count and stock status.
    • The scraper does not start until tos_reviewed: true is set in the configuration, which enforces a review of each site's Terms of Use.

    Data cleaning

    • Rows with a null or zero price, a selling price above MRP, or an exact duplicate are removed. Discount percentage is always recomputed and never taken from the page.
    • Products are identified by URL rather than name. Review counts that decrease are treated as noise and carried forward.
    • A missing struck-through price is recorded as "not discounted" and flagged, so that a broken selector cannot appear as a genuine 0% discount.

    Analysis

    • Events are discount changes. The first snapshot of each product is a baseline and is never counted as a change.
    • Seven-day before and after windows are anchored to the two snapshots that straddle each event, so the change itself does not leak into the "before" window.
    • Events with an internal gap of more than three days in either window are excluded, because an outage is not equivalent to an absence of reviews.
    • The discount floor is estimated with a hinge model and a permutation p-value, which prevents the search over thresholds from inflating significance.
    • Competitive response lag excludes right-censored events, meaning price cuts for which no follow-up could be observed within the horizon.

    Limitations

    • Review velocity is not sales. It measures growth in the number of ratings, which is lagged and noisy, and not every buyer leaves a rating.
    • The before-and-after design removes each product's baseline but does not control for seasonality or competitor activity.
    • A 28-day window is too short to capture a full sale-event cycle.
    • Cross-platform matching is manual and may mis-match bundles or regional variants.
